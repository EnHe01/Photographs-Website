It’s been a long time since I’ve been to Hokkaido.

The last time I was there, I went skiing in Niseko, Sapporo. Even though I was still quite young, I clearly remember the heavy snowfall that fell day and night without stopping. Every day when I opened the door, it seemed like I could see walls of snow taller than a person—a vast expanse of white stretching out before my eyes. That was the impression Hokkaido left on me back then: “caves” carved out of the snow with shovels at the entrances of buildings, black granules scattered all over the sidewalks, and snowdrifts of unknown depth everywhere—so deep that even when I stuck my whole arm inside, I couldn’t reach the bottom.

This trip to Hokkaido, however, came about by a twist of fate. I consider myself very lucky to have made so many friends in such a short time since arriving in Japan. I had just moved to Tokyo to start school, and during my first winter break, I casually mentioned to them that I might be planning a trip to Hokkaido. Someone enthusiastically asked if I wanted to join them, and before long, I was on a plane headed for Hokkaido.

![]()

The long-missed cold air filled my lungs, like a baptism that cleansed the confusion of the past few months. We then wandered through the streets, climbed small mounds of snow, and looked down on the city from the hotel’s high-rise: the snow-capped mountains in the distance, the roads in the middle, and the layered buildings up close—all covered in pristine white snow.

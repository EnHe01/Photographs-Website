The moment the plane touched down, the view out the window was the familiar grayish-white landscape. Snow was piled high on both sides of the runway at New Chitose, heaped so low it looked as if it might cover something at any moment. The long-missed cold air filled my lungs like a baptism, washing away all the confusion I’d felt in Tokyo over the past few months.

![DSC_2433.jpeg](/uploads/4cdeb91032f8583f5c791baa719e701b.jpg)

This was a trip that came about by a twist of fate. I had only recently arrived in Tokyo to start school, and during my first winter break, I casually mentioned to some new friends that I might go to Hokkaido. Someone enthusiastically asked if I wanted to join them. Before long, I was on a plane. **I** considered myself lucky—to have met so many kind friends in such a short time since arriving in Japan.

Walking through the streets, crossing small hills of snow, and looking down on the city from the high-rise hotel: the snow-capped mountains in the distance, the roads in the middle, and the layered buildings up close—everything was blanketed in pristine white snow. Suddenly, I recalled my last visit to Hokkaido, when I went skiing in Niseko. Though I was still quite young at the time, I clearly remember the heavy snowfall that fell day and night without ceasing. Every day when I opened the door, I’d see the “cave” shoveled out of the snow at the entrance, the black granules scattered across the sidewalk, and snowdrifts of unknown depth everywhere—so deep that even when I stuck my whole arm in, I couldn’t reach the bottom. That was the impression Hokkaido left on me back then: rugged, bountiful, and uncompromising.

![IMG_3982.jpeg](/uploads/392b3759159bafc709dd7fce1b8c4b1a.jpg)
\*Niseko

This time, however, it has mellowed considerably.

**![e4659126a5003cd80bcbfcbaa6aee4bd.jpeg](/uploads/eab328e195c180ee9edcc16499031762.jpg)**

I went to Otaru. The sea was as still as a mirror that had been set aside for ages—reflecting nothing, nor did it seem to want to. A red and a white lighthouse stood guard at either end of the harbor, with a vast expanse of gray-blue in between; the sky and the water had long since made peace, too lazy even to draw a clear boundary.

![7f204dd5da1277be31995ac6496d5b2f.jpeg](/uploads/00c9437d14cd47880c53938dce91c11d.jpg)

There are rickshaws on the streets of Otaru. The rickshaw pullers, hunched over, push their empty carts along the icy road, with snow piled high on both sides, towering above the wheels.

![67a856bde95654810e98178bf19ffbfc.jpeg](/uploads/997177d5b680d924ac54985c50ef8fbc.jpg)

The cemetery designed by Tadao Ando lies in the snowy expanse. Passing through the arched entrance felt like being swallowed up and then spat out—backlit, everyone became a silhouette; some walked on, others paused. Upon emerging, the sky was still the same sky, the snow still the same snow, yet suddenly everything seemed a size larger.

![cfe83b1b1e72c76ed28a42e6e693e82a.jpeg](/uploads/ada1fd3fc4e471659a8fba0f0b78a6f7.jpg)

Walking further in, there was a giant Buddha. When I looked up, its face filled the entire sky. Snow lay piled on its head; its eyes were closed—seeing nothing, yet seeing everything.

![2e01b45b42b491bbd7554496464009c5.jpeg](/uploads/3807fbc261d22c2309d3b1f85ec994fa.jpg)

The snow was deep enough. Someone fell backward and sank halfway into it, limbs pointing skyward. Against the black-and-white snowy landscape, the scene was surprisingly lighthearted—after all, when you’re alive, you’re bound to get a little silly like this.

![0b2f83989868d6f6f7bf7c98978001db.jpeg](/uploads/52974cd3db6832ff4b46f3cc4c925921.jpg)

! [60772946ac059a8715f9140333b2c0ac.jpeg](/uploads/36c1037931279a242033303273c3b41c.jpg)

The stone statue of Kṣitigarbha by the roadside stood half-buried in the snow, right next to a stone stele inscribed with “Namo Amitabha Buddha.” Who knows how many winters it has stood there—yet it shows no signs of aging. Twilight began to gather on the horizon—pink and gray hues, layer upon layer—and in the blink of an eye, it would fade away.

![d447aa462a839d724f52bcd851a37c67.jpeg](/uploads/679452cb5f6f299f1c79fbd6f36ede43.jpg)

The moai statues stand in a straight line, varying in size, their profiles all facing the same direction. A deer appears on the snowy slope, gazing back at them from afar. The orange horizon grows darker bit by bit.

![988e5f40ec1c3ab33edcafc2093e5a09.jpeg](/uploads/3b538b22569ef44d40376b683118df58.jpg)

Winter in Hokkaido doesn’t give you much. But what it does give is authentic.

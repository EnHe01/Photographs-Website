Busan is a small city nestled on a hillside. Buildings crowd together, climbing all the way from the seashore up to the mountainside. Power lines in the alleys slice through the sky, while seagulls look down from above at this city that looks as if it had been squeezed in someone’s fist, then return to the rocks with the tide.

![](/uploads/dscf0909.jpeg)

I’ve been to Busan a few times.

Before the pandemic, there was a certain vibrancy here: the smoke from the restaurants on Haeundae’s shopping street, the bustling voices of the crowd, the scent of the sea mingling with the aroma of food, and the shouts of the owner of the skewered meat stall right beside me… It was noisy and unrefined, yet it felt very comfortable—I never grew tired of it.

But coming back this time, I felt something was missing.

![](/uploads/dscf0951.jpeg)

What exactly was it? It’s the shops that once lined the street, their windows plastered with faded “For Sale” signs—some that have been there for ages, the edges curling up yet left untouched. Shops where I once enjoyed lively conversations with the owners are now gone, and the once-bustling streets have become deserted. Perhaps it’s just the off-season.

Busan has never been South Korea’s most dazzling city. But its port, its fish market, and its hillsides have always been there. It’s rugged, and it’s precisely this ruggedness that defines Busan, making it a city with a unique character.

![](/uploads/dscf0920.jpeg)

I walked from Haeundae to Mipo Station and took a capsule car to Cheongsapo. Although the journey was only a little over two kilometers, it felt like it took a long time.

![](/uploads/dscf1157.jpeg)

As soon as you arrive at Cheongsapo, you’ll see two lighthouses beneath the tracks. People often sit on the seawall fishing, and occasionally children play nearby. This tranquility is mesmerizing; you’re drawn into that mood without needing to say a word, allowing you to savor the moment.

![](/uploads/dscf1178.jpeg)

At 4:00 a.m. the next morning, I walked out onto the beach and found the scenery completely different from the day before. The sea hadn’t yet awakened; the sky was just beginning to lighten, holding back a sliver of the rising sun—a density of atmosphere you could almost touch.

![](/uploads/dscf1285.jpeg)

Past noon, Haeundae Beach

![](/uploads/dscf1116.jpeg)

Busan is an interesting place.
